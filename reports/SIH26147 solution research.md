# Rebuild the plan around one GF(2) kernel

> **Status (27 September 2026).** This report was written against the earlier, phase-based plan. Its recommendations have since been folded into [PLAN.md](../docs/PLAN.md), which is now organised as milestones M0–M8; the [table at the end](#prioritised-changes-to-our-plan-documents) records where each one landed. In the body, "PLAN.md Phase N" means the old plan: Phase 1 → M0–M1, Phase 2 → M2 plus the sync part of M3, Phase 3 → M4, Phase 4 → M3, Phase 5 → M5, Phase 6 → M6, Phase 7 → M0 (UI), M2 (tiles), M7 and M8 (packaging), Phase 8 → M8. Where this report differs from PLAN, STANDARDS or the README, those documents are current.

Our SIH26147 plan is sound in architecture. Research from 2022 to 2026 suggests five kinds of change to its content. First, **the blind FEC and interleaver stage should rest on one Numba-accelerated, bit-packed GF(2) Gaussian-elimination kernel.** Around it sit soft-decision scoring (reliability-ordered GJETP, syndrome posterior probability), a Galois-Field Fourier Transform test for Reed-Solomon, and a catalogue of *standard* pseudo-random interleaver generators in place of generic "PRNG seed search". We found no open-source implementation of any of these, so a working, benchmarked one is itself a differentiator. Second, **our FEC-identification target of "≥ 95% at channel BER ≤ 3%" cannot be met as written for long codes.** Hard-decision rank methods need error-free rows, and at 3% BER a 2,040-bit RS(255,223) row is almost never clean. Targets must be set per code family and stated as soft-decision targets. Third, **AMC should switch from an O'Shea-style ResNet to a roughly 10K-parameter complex-as-real 1-D CNN.** Such models match 400K-parameter models on RadioML. They should be trained on our own impaired generator, because RadioML has documented label and SNR flaws, and they should use a layered open-set rejector (SNR gate, then energy score, then prototype distance). Fourth, we should borrow IQEngine's MIT-licensed web architecture and serve a tiled STFT pyramid to WebGL, and ship with PyInstaller in one-folder mode, with a pinned Numba build and a writable Numba cache directory. Fifth, the Indian over-the-air plan must change on two counts. The Telecommunications Act 2023 makes *possession* of radio equipment an authorisation question, which the README does not mention. Some planned ground truth (NOAA APT, FM RDS) may not exist in India in 2026. Remote Indian KiwiSDRs recording DGLL NAVTEX and HF signals are the lowest-risk source of Indian recordings.

## One GF(2) rank kernel covers every blind code and interleaver test

The literature converges on a single primitive. Write the intercepted bitstream into a matrix at a candidate width, run Gauss-Jordan elimination over GF(2) (GJETP), and look for rank deficiency or dual-code (parity-check) vectors. This one step recovers convolutional code length n, the dual-code basis and the generator in three stages ([Su et al. 2014, PMC4055125](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4055125/)). It also recovers punctured convolutional encoders as an equivalent code followed by a mother-code-plus-puncturing search ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/260648020_Algebraic_method_for_blind_recovery_of_punctured_convolutional_encoders_from_an_erroneous_bitstream); [Physical Communication 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1874490721000343)). Block-interleaver period and synchronisation fall out of the same scan: normalised rank drops when the analysis width is a multiple of the period, and drops most at the correct sync ([Sicot, Houcke & Barbier](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1016/j.sigpro.2008.09.012)). PLAN.md Phase 5 lists "dual-code parity checks / GF(2) rank", "stride/rank scans" and "delay structure" as separate techniques. **They should become one kernel with thin loops around it:** a uint64 bit-packed elimination returning rank and kernel, reused for code length, sync offset, interleaver period, puncturing period and parity-check vectors.

The hard-decision version has a ceiling that our targets ignore. A row of l bits is error-free with probability (1−p)^l. At p = 1% and l ≈ 100, only about 37% of rows are clean. That is why the literature moves to soft reliability ordering, which pivots on the most reliable rows first ([PMC4055125](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4055125/)). It also moves to rank iteration, which repeats GJETP until the measured rank approaches the noiseless rank ([IEEE Comm. Letters 2023](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/369308477_A_Rank-iteration_Based_Blind_Identification_Method_for_Convolutional_Codes)), and to correlation attacks with a 6–8σ acceptance threshold ([PMC4055125](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4055125/)). The same arithmetic shows why STANDARDS §8's "≥ 95% FEC-ID at channel BER ≤ 3%" needs rewording. For a K=7 rate-½ code scanned at roughly 40-bit widths, 0.97^40 ≈ 0.30, so enough rows are clean and the target is plausible. For RS(255,223) seen as a 2,040-bit binary code, 0.97^2040 ≈ 10⁻²⁷, so a hard-decision rank scan is hopeless. In a concatenated chain the RS stage sees post-Viterbi BER, not channel BER. **The target should become a per-family table** (conv/punctured at raw channel BER, RS at post-inner-decoder BER, LDPC at Es/N0), measured with soft inputs. The 2026 closed-form analysis of rank-based rate recovery gives a theoretical basis for choosing the observation size L ([Vedantam & Ganti, arXiv 2603.02031](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02031)).

For Reed-Solomon, PLAN.md says "RS by syndrome at each alignment". Current practice is stronger and more specific. First, a binary rank scan finds n·m and bit alignment (reduced row echelon form, plus fault-tolerant decomposition when errors are present). Then a GFFT is computed for each candidate primitive polynomial. The run of spectral indices that are always zero gives n−k and the first consecutive root ([RS identification via probability statistics and GFFT](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/331721453_Parameter_Identification_of_Reed-Solomon_Codes_Based_on_Probability_Statistics_and_Galois_Field_Fourier_Transform)). A 2023 low-complexity GFFT variant reports over 95% recognition and at least a 7 dB SNR gain over earlier algorithms, although that figure comes from the abstract only ([IEEE 10149421](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10149421)). The search space is small: 16 degree-8 primitive polynomials × 8 symbol offsets, plus a CCSDS dual-basis hypothesis. `galois` (MIT) already provides the field arithmetic and RS codec ([galois](https://github.com/mhostetter/galois)).

For LDPC, the catalogue approach in the plan is right, but the test should be **soft**. The syndrome posterior probability (the likelihood that all of a candidate's checks are satisfied, computed from LLRs with the tanh rule) is the established blind catalogue test ([Moosavi & Larsson via arXiv 2606.17705](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.17705)). Reconstructing sparse H from scratch degrades at low SNR for long codes and stays research-grade ([IET 2023](https://ietresearch.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1049/cmu2.12552)).

Interleavers need three specific changes. **Convolutional (Forney) interleavers** should be found by a grid over (branches I, depth J, commutator phase), each candidate checked by RS rank or GFFT zeros on the de-interleaved stream. Low-complexity GJETP estimators for exactly this exist ([Sci. China 2013](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11432-012-4673-9)). **Helical interleavers** have dedicated methods that recover period, rows and columns ([IEEE 6174276](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/6174276/); [IEEE 7410351](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/7410351/)). **Pseudo-random interleavers** are the biggest wording change. The README promises to "search known PRNG families over a bounded seed range", but no 2022–2026 work on generic seed search was found. Full permutation recovery exists only as advanced research for turbo codes and interleaved convolutional codes ([Cluzeau, Finiasz & Tillich, arXiv 1006.0259](https://arxiv.org/abs/1006.0259); [Tixier, arXiv 1501.03715](https://arxiv.org/abs/1501.03715)). The defensible replacement is a **catalogue of standard permutations**: the 3GPP turbo interleaver, the LTE QPP f1/f2 table, the 802.11 bit interleaver and the DVB-S2 column-twist interleaver. Each is tested by de-interleaving and then checking for code structure. Anything else is reported UNKNOWN with its measured period. For period detection itself, the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test on rank distributions beats earlier Bernoulli and KL-divergence criteria, and it yields a probability under a random-data null ([Wee, Choi & Jeong 2021, PMC8155855](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8155855/)).

On false accepts, the plan's Holm/Bonferroni correction goes further than the literature, which almost never states an explicit multiple-comparison correction. Instead it relies on large-σ thresholds and multinomial or KS nulls ([PMC4055125](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4055125/); [PMC8155855](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8155855/)). Keep our correction, and add two cheap safeguards. First, for a random L×w matrix, the chance of any rank deficiency is about 2^−(L−w+1). **Enforcing L ≥ w + 30 therefore makes a chance rank drop negligible even across 10⁴ hypotheses.** Second, re-run every detector on bit-shuffled copies of the input to measure its empirical false-alarm rate, and show that rate in the D8 hypothesis table.

Deep learning belongs only in a supporting role. The dual-branch DBFCNN reaches 98% at BER < 10⁻³ and above 85% at BER 0.1 across seven code families ([Ma et al. 2026, PMC12881466](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12881466/)). ConvLSTM models classify 17 convolutional code classes at 98.7% average ([PMC11858941](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11858941/)). These are closed-set classifiers, though. They recover no generators, and none of these papers publishes its code. One 2025 DL detector for convolutional codes was **withdrawn** because its noise model was wrong ([arXiv 2501.11487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.11487)). The practical takeaway is to reuse DBFCNN's handcrafted features (run length, entropy, autocorrelation, rank features) in a gradient-boosted "code family" pre-classifier. It is cheap and explainable, and it orders the algebraic search rather than replacing it. **No public repository implements GJETP, GFFT RS recognition or interleaver rank scans**. Our GitHub searches returned only blind *channel*-identification and modulation-recognition repos, such as [LiuRuiQi/Blind-Channel-Identification](https://github.com/LiuRuiQi/Blind-Channel-Identification). An open, tested implementation is therefore a differentiator the STANDARDS matrix should claim as D9.

## Ten-thousand-parameter CNNs, fixed data and layered rejection beat a bigger AMC model

PLAN.md Phase 3 specifies an "O'Shea-style ResNet trained on RadioML 2018.01A plus TorchSig". The 2025 benchmarks favour much smaller models. **LDCVNN (9–11K parameters, 0.6–6.6 MFLOPs) scores 62.4% averaged over SNR and 92.4% peak on RML2016.10a, and 60.2% / 96.1% on RML2018.01A. MCLDNN, with about 406K parameters and 48.7 MFLOPs, scores 60.8% / 90.8% on RML2016.10a** ([LDCVNN, PMC12031408](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12031408/)). Capacity is not the bottleneck; the low-SNR bins and QAM16/64 confusion are. STANDARDS §8's RadioML target (≥ 55% averaged, stretch 62%) is therefore realistic for a CPU-sized model. MobileRaT's 65.9% average sets the ceiling ([MobileRaT, Drones 2023](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023Drone...7..596Z/abstract)). Complex layers must be written as paired real Conv1d layers, because ONNX runtimes largely lack complex types. FFT, atan2 and unwrap should be computed in NumPy before the graph ([ONNX Runtime quantization docs](https://github.com/microsoft/onnxruntime/blob/gh-pages/docs/performance/model-optimizations/quantization.md)). At this size FP32 ONNX is fast enough. Skip INT8: quantisation shifts logits and breaks calibrated OOD thresholds.

The training data needs more attention than the model. RadioML has documented defects. Its SNR labels are "off by tens of dB" (label 18 measured about 40 dB in-band). AM-SSB in 2016.10a/b is pure noise. About half of the label −2 PSK/QAM examples look like noise only. The 2018.01A class-name mapping does not match the paper ([Spooner, CSP blog](https://cyclostationary.blog/2020/08/17/more-on-deepsigs-rml-data-sets/)). **RadioML should be kept as a public comparison benchmark for D1, with the label map corrected and AM-SSB dropped. It should not be the primary training set or the source of SNR truth.** Its CC BY-NC-SA licence is a second reason ([sofwerx mirror](https://github.com/sofwerx/deepsig_datasets)). The domain-gap evidence is stark. A model at 95.65% in-domain fell to 34.55% on USRP over-the-air captures. Unsupervised domain adaptation recovered 87.2% (STAR) or 61.5% (CORAL), and moderately distorted training data transferred best ([arXiv 2510.00589](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.00589)). The plan's impairment harness should add random resampling and sps jitter, IQ imbalance, Rayleigh fading and random phase and time shifts, the set Sig53 uses ([arXiv 2207.09918](https://arxiv.org/html/2207.09918v1)). It should also add AGC gain and noise-only and partial-burst frames as an explicit noise class. CORAL fine-tuning should be run once our own Indian captures exist.

Variable symbol rate is handled by estimating Rs first, resampling to a canonical 4–8 sps, and training with ±10–20% jitter. Random resampling is a standard Sig53 impairment for this reason ([arXiv 2207.09918](https://arxiv.org/html/2207.09918v1)). Averaging log-probabilities over many windows then lifts low-SNR accuracy on long captures. Inputs should stack [I, Q, |x|, Δphase], and a small cumulant and spectral-line vector (C40, C42, C6x, σ_aa, σ_dp, the x²/x⁴ line peaks) should be concatenated before the final layer. That fusion is established ([Sensors 21(6):2117](https://doi.org/10.3390/s21062117)), though higher-order cumulants discriminate poorly between 16QAM and 64QAM. A PET-CGDNN-style offset-correction front end trades little accuracy for speed ([PMC11679410](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11679410/)).

For open-set operation, the literature evaluates known-class accuracy, unknown rejection, AUROC, normalised accuracy and openness-controlled held-out splits. Reconstruction methods such as CIR edge out OpenMax at −2 dB (AUS 0.899 vs 0.892), but those results are on radar waveforms ([arXiv 2312.13023](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.13023v1)). Per-class distance thresholding in logit space works for few-shot open-set AMC ([arXiv 2410.10265](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10265v2)), and the energy score is parameter-free ([Liu et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.03759)). **The plan's single "noise/unknown class" should become three layers: an SNR/energy gate, a temperature-scaled energy score, and Mahalanobis distance to class prototypes, with per-class thresholds at 95% TPR.** Evaluation should hold out, for example, 8PSK, GFSK and 256QAM, and report AUROC, FPR@95TPR and OSCR *per SNR bin*. Temperature scaling reduces miscalibration without changing the argmax ([arXiv 1909.13550](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13550)). The plan's "only if ECE improves" rule is right, and ECE should be reported per SNR bin. Real-world open-set numbers run far below simulation: a sea trial reached 64.1% known accuracy and 74.4% rejection ([JASA](https://pubs.aip.org/asa/jasa/article-abstract/159/5/4129/3391280/Open-set-modulation-recognition-for-underwater)). The "≥ 99% noise/unknown rejection" target should cover noise only, with a separate and more modest target for unseen modulations.

TorchSig remains the right generator but is heavier than the plan assumes. It is MIT-licensed. Version 2.x renamed Sig53 to "Narrowband" (61 signals). Version 2.2 added DVB-S2, GSM, 802.11a, LoRa and ADS-B, and moved pretrained models to a separate repo. It recommends Ubuntu 22.04+, 4+ cores and **about 1 TB of storage**, with no pre-generated downloads ([TorchSig releases](https://github.com/TorchDSP/torchsig/releases); [TorchSig README](https://github.com/TorchDSP/torchsig)). A lean NumPy generator for the in-scope PSK/QAM/FSK classes should be our main source. TorchSig should serve as the *independent* generator the STANDARDS "in-scope" target requires.

## Blind estimation is mature. Format sniffing and sample-rate inference are open.

Symbol-rate estimation should be layered. The |x|² spectral line (Oerder–Meyr, feedforward, insensitive to frequency offset) comes first, with x²/x⁴ lines for BPSK and QPSK. A fine cyclic-autocorrelation search refines the estimate, and an all-alpha FAM or SSCA pass confirms it ([Optica OE](https://opg.optica.org/oe/fulltext.cfm?uri=oe-22-6-6749&id=281906); [CSP blog](https://cyclostationary.blog/2022/11/17/csp-community-spotlight-a-publicly-available-python-based-scf-estimator/)). The documented failure mode is small roll-off: cyclic estimators "may totally fail when the roll-off factor approaches zero" ([arXiv 2001.01692](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.01692)). Below β ≈ 0.1 the tool should fall back to occupied bandwidth and mark the result low-confidence. FSK needs the instantaneous-frequency path, because its constant envelope gives no |x|² line ([CSP blog on FSK](https://cyclostationary.blog/2023/04/25/cyclostationarity-of-frequency-shift-keyed-signals/)). Roll-off, which PLAN.md does not estimate yet, costs little: a least-squares fit of the RRC PSD, or the IFFT peak-ratio method ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/224288947_Blind_Roll-off_Factor_and_Symbol_Rate_Estimation_using_IFFT_and_Least_Squares_Estimator)). The result snaps to the standard values 0.2, 0.25, 0.35 and 0.5, and cyclic harmonics distinguish rectangular from RRC pulses ([PySDR](https://pysdr.org/content/cyclostationary.html)). For SNR, M2M4 is strictly valid only for constant-modulus PSK ([PMC8347679](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8347679/)), and eigenvalue/MDL estimators overestimate signal dimension at low SNR ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/270897440_Samples_Covariance_Matrix_Eigenvalues_Based_Blind_SNR_Estimation)). The plan should report a PSD in-band-versus-guard estimate, M2M4 and MDL together, and use their agreement as the confidence value. The MDL count doubles as a "more than one source" hint.

Two ingest problems have no published solution, which is both a risk and an opening. **No common tool infers raw IQ datatype or endianness automatically.** URH and the usual guidance rely on file extensions or SigMF ([URH guide](https://www.oldergeeks.com/downloads/files/userguide.pdf); [PySDR IQ files](https://pysdr.org/content/iq_files)), and only one rival has an 11-criterion scorer (STANDARDS §5.3). The planned sniffer should score every hypothesis (u8, i8, i16/i32/f32 in both byte orders, f64, interleaved or planar) on five things: float validity, spectral non-whiteness, lag-1 autocorrelation, I/Q power balance and DC cleanliness. It should report the margin over the runner-up and be validated with a confusion matrix over all format permutations. **An IQ-versus-QI swap cannot be decided from the data**, because it only mirrors the spectrum. It must stay an analyst toggle, backed by priors such as the side a pilot or carrier sits on. Sample rate is likewise unidentifiable from the samples alone ([PySDR](https://pysdr.org/content/iq_files)). The honest design parses the filename and the WAV `auxi` chunk, snaps to device rate lists, and ranks candidates with known standards. RTL-SDR tops out at 3.2 MS/s ([RTLSDR-Airband wiki](https://github.com/szpajder/RTLSDR-Airband/wiki/Tweaking-sampling-rate-and-FFT-size)), and HackRF uses fixed filter-related rates up to 20 MS/s ([HackRF docs](https://hackrf.readthedocs.io/en/latest/sampling_rate.html)). If a recognised symbol rate × candidate Fs matches within 0.1%, that candidate is promoted to HYPOTHESIS. The README's "cannot be recovered" statement should soften to "never assumed; ranked candidates, promoted only by structural matches".

For wideband detection, the plan's CFAR pipeline matches accepted practice. Morphological thresholding gives approximately CFAR behaviour in coloured noise ([MATEC 2021](https://www.matec-conferences.org/articles/matecconf/pdf/2021/05/matecconf_cscns20_04011.pdf)), and CA-CFAR runs on low-cost SDRs ([PMC13417223](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13417223/)). Three additions matter for D7. First, run the detector at two or three FFT sizes and fuse the results with NMS, so short hops and weak narrowband signals are both caught. Second, turn frequency-hopping into DBSCAN clustering of detection boxes by bandwidth, dwell and power, then estimate hop rate and hop set. Published FH methods are papers only, with no Python tracker ([DTIC ADA339336](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/tr/pdf/ADA339336.pdf)). Third, handle co-channel overlap by *detecting* it (two distinct cyclic lines or M-th-power lines, or MDL > 1), not by separating it. Dedicated cyclic-correlation estimators exist for overlapped PCMA signals ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/349402147_Blind_Symbol_Rate_and_Frequency_Offset_Estimation_for_PCMA_Signals_via_Cyclic_Correlations)). An ML cross-check is available: gr-spectrumdetect's YOLO11-S detector is MIT-licensed and trained on TorchSig wideband spectrograms ([gr-spectrumdetect](https://github.com/TorchDSP/gr-spectrumdetect)). It could run via ONNX Runtime without shipping the Ultralytics package, whose AGPL licence we believe applies but have not confirmed. Its CPU latency is undocumented, so it stays optional.

## IQEngine, galois and a pinned Numba build define the shippable stack

The library picture confirms the plan's permissive core. There are three corrections and one version pin. **numpy 2.5 requires Python ≥ 3.12** ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/pypi/numpy/json)), and onnxruntime 1.30 ships Windows wheels for 3.11–3.14 ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/onnxruntime/)), so the stack should pin CPython 3.12 exactly. `galois` 0.4.11 (MIT, Numba-backed, with RS, BCH, LFSRs and GF polynomials) should be our GF and RS engine, not just a cross-check ([PyPI galois](https://pypi.org/project/galois/)). scikit-commpy has had no release since October 2022 and pyldpc none since February 2020 ([PyPI CommPy](https://pypi.org/project/scikit-commpy/); [PyPI pyldpc](https://pypi.org/pypi/pyldpc/json)). The README should call them vendored references, not dependencies, and STANDARDS §3 should list pyldpc as MIT, not BSD. `komm` is the most active Python comms toolbox but is **GPL-3.0-only** ([PyPI komm](https://pypi.org/project/komm/)), so it may be used only as a dev-time oracle. Sionna 2.x now pulls in PyTorch and targets Ubuntu ([PyPI sionna](https://pypi.org/project/sionna/)). PySDR's code is **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0** ([PySDR](https://pysdr.org/content/cyclostationary.html)), so our FAM/SSCA must be written from the papers, not pasted. STANDARDS §3 currently lists PySDR only as "CC". No maintained permissive cyclostationary package exists; CycloTorch is GPU/PyTorch and not on PyPI ([SoftwareX 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352711026001901)).

For the GUI, **IQEngine is almost exactly our target architecture**: MIT-licensed, React/TypeScript over a Python FastAPI API, SigMF-native, with its own MIT `webfft` package ([IQEngine](https://github.com/IQEngine/IQEngine); [npm webfft](https://registry.npmjs.org/webfft)). It should move in STANDARDS §3 from "UX reference" to "component source", with its cloud and blob-storage parts stripped out. OpenWebRX is AGPL-3.0 and may serve as a behavioural reference only ([OpenWebRX](https://github.com/jketterl/openwebrx)). The rendering design should become concrete. The server computes a multi-resolution STFT pyramid quantised to uint8 dB and serves tiles by (level, time, frequency). The client uploads each tile as an R8 WebGL2 texture and applies the colormap through a 256-entry LUT shader, so contrast changes need no re-fetch. deck.gl's TileLayer (MIT, 9.4.0) or raw regl can manage level of detail ([npm deck.gl](https://registry.npmjs.org/deck.gl)). This is also what makes D6 (multi-GB streaming) visible in the browser. PSD and time plots should use uPlot with min/max decimation ([npm uplot](https://registry.npmjs.org/uplot)). Constellations above about 10⁶ points should switch to a server-side `histogram2d` density texture. SCF images must be max-pooled on the server, because FAM output has "an enormous number of pixels" ([PySDR](https://pysdr.org/content/cyclostationary.html)).

Packaging is where rivals will stumble, and Numba is the trap. Frozen numba/llvmlite builds have failed with missing `numba.core.*` modules and WinError 126 DLL errors under PyInstaller ([pyinstaller #8989](https://github.com/pyinstaller/pyinstaller/issues/8989); [pyinstaller #2180](https://github.com/pyinstaller/pyinstaller/issues/2180)). PLAN.md Phase 7 should specify PyInstaller 6.x in `--onedir` mode ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/pypi/pyinstaller/json)). It should also pin a tested numba/llvmlite pair, add hidden imports, set `NUMBA_CACHE_DIR` to a writable user directory and pre-warm the kernels at first launch. The wheelhouse should be built with `pip download --only-binary=:all:` for win_amd64 and manylinux. pywebview (BSD) is an optional desktop shell ([PyPI pywebview](https://pypi.org/pypi/pywebview/json)). Nuitka's compiler is AGPL-3.0 ([PyPI nuitka](https://pypi.org/pypi/nuitka/json)); avoid it unless its output exception is confirmed.

Reference decoders give protocol-level ground truth, but nearly all are GPL. rtl_433 (GPL-2.0) reads cu8/cs16/cf32 files and emits JSON ([rtl_433](https://github.com/merbanan/rtl_433)). readsb, AIS-catcher and SatDump are GPL-3.0, with file input and JSON, NMEA or CLI pipelines ([readsb](https://github.com/wiedehopf/readsb); [AIS-catcher](https://github.com/jvde-github/AIS-catcher); [SatDump](https://github.com/SatDump/SatDump)). multimon-ng is GPL-2.0 ([multimon-ng](https://github.com/EliasOenal/multimon-ng)). Only redsea (RDS) is MIT ([redsea](https://github.com/windytan/redsea)). **These should run as subprocesses in a `bench/decoder_truth/` harness, never linked into the product.** A new `decoder-truth` project skill in CLAUDE_SKILLS_MCP.md §3 should wrap them and write CRC-valid frame counts into SigMF annotations.

## Indian recordings need NAVTEX, KiwiSDR and a possession-law caveat

Public IQ sources are fewer and more restricted than D4 assumes. SigMF recordings carry a per-file `core:license`, which makes IQEngine's example archive the cleanest source ([SigMF](https://github.com/sigmf/SigMF); [iqengine.org](https://iqengine.org/browser)). The LakeShark corpus (CC0/CC-BY, cu8 SigMF, built for decoder testing) **was empty when checked**, but it invites ADS-B and POCSAG contributions, which makes it a natural place to publish our own captures ([LakeShark](https://github.com/SAMS0N1TE/LakeShark-Signal-Corpus)). DeepSig and WiSig are CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 ([DeepSig](https://www.deepsig.ai/datasets/); [WiSig](https://cores.ee.ucla.edu/wisig/license/)). NIST's CBRS radar set is public but synthetic ([NIST](https://data.nist.gov/od/id/mds2-2116)). SatNOGS does not upload IQ, only CC BY-SA audio, waterfalls and CRC-checked frames ([SatNOGS wiki](https://wiki.satnogs.org/Artifacts)); the CAMRAS Dwingeloo archive is an IQ exception ([CAMRAS](https://data.camras.nl/satnogs/)). sigidwiki/Artemis provides OGG audio and PNG images, not IQ ([Artemis-DB](https://github.com/AresValley/Artemis-DB)). ORACLE is a useful loader test, because its SigMF metadata declares 32-bit samples while the data is complex128 ([GENESYS ORACLE](https://www.genesys-lab.org/oracle)). Our ingest tests should include a file-size-versus-datatype consistency check for exactly this case.

The best Indian ground truth is maritime HF. DGLL runs seven NAVTEX stations (Veraval, Vengurla Point, Muttam Point, Porto Novo, Vakalpudi, Balasore and Keating Point in the Andamans). They broadcast in English on 518 kHz and in local languages on 490 kHz, on a published four-hourly UTC schedule with fixed station letters ([DGLL NAVTEX](https://www.dgll.nic.in/about-DGLL/Service-reminders/navtex)). A recording therefore comes with a known time, station ID and FSK/SITOR-B structure to verify against. Public KiwiSDRs operate in Bangalore ([Skywave Linux](https://skywavelinux.com/websdr-mediumwave-list.html)). `kiwirecorder.py -m iq --kiwi-wav` writes GNSS-timestamped IQ WAV ([kiwiclient](https://github.com/jks-prv/kiwiclient/blob/master/README.md)), but the bandwidth is about 12–20 kHz, so KiwiSDR suits HF only ([KiwiSDR forum](https://forum.kiwisdr.com/index.php?p=%2Fdiscussion%2F1450%2Frecording-iq-data-with-gt-20khz-bandwidth-from-a-single-kiwisdr)). For VHF/UHF, the RTL-SDR Blog V4 sells in India for about ₹6,000 ([Robu.in](https://robu.in/product/rtl-sdr-blog-v4-r828d-rtl2832u-1ppm-tcxo-sma-software-defined-radio-with-dipole-antenna-kit/); [price history](https://pricehistory.app/p/rtl-sdr-blog-v4-r828d-rtl2832u-1ppm-tWbCvAIm)). PLAN.md Phase 8 needs two edits. NOAA APT should become conditional, because POES decommissioning status in 2026 is unverified, and Meteor-M LRPT should become the primary satellite target. FM RDS should also be conditional, because we found no evidence of consistent RDS use in India. The 19 kHz pilot and stereo structure make safer FM ground truth.

The legal framing in the README is incomplete. It cites Section 20 (interception) but not **Section 3 of the Telecommunications Act 2023, which requires authorisation to "possess radio equipment"** ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/broadcasting-film-tv-radio/1625116/dot-releases-new-radio-equipment-possession-authorization-rules)). The Act repealed the Wireless Telegraphy Act 1933 ([Act text](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/20101/1/A2023-44.pdf)). The draft Radio Equipment Possession Authorisation Rules of 27 February 2025 state that "no person shall possess radio equipment" without authorisation unless exempted under rule 4 ([TeamLease RegTech](https://www.teamleaseregtech.com/updates/article/40042/draft-telecommunications-radio-equipment-possession-authorisation-rule/)). We could not find the final exemption list, so whether a student SDR is exempt is genuinely unknown. The plan should rank sources by legal risk. Public datasets and remote KiwiSDRs come first. Local receive-only captures come second, done under the college's institutional umbrella, limited to broadcast and safety signals (FM, NAVTEX, ADS-B, AIS, weather satellites), and never airband, encrypted or military traffic. The team should also ask the NTRO organisers whether they will supply sample recordings.

## Conclusion

The research moves the competitive argument away from breadth. The field already treats Viterbi, RS, LDPC and four interleaver families as table stakes, and 2022–2026 deep-learning work shows closed-set code classifiers are easy to publish and impossible to verify. What remains scarce is *recoverable parameters with a measured false-alarm rate*. That is an algebraic and statistical problem that one well-engineered GF(2) kernel serves end to end, and no one has published an implementation of it. The same logic applies to AMC: tiny models perform at parity, so rigour in the data, the open-set evaluation and the per-SNR reporting matter more than model size.

The biggest risks sit in claims the plan makes today. These are a 3% BER target that algebra rules out for long codes, a pseudo-random seed search with no supporting method, Indian ground truth (APT, RDS) that may not be on air, and a legal note that omits possession. Correcting these before 30 September costs nothing and turns them into credibility, which judges tend to reward over coverage.
